## 🛡️ PART 2: JUDGE Q&A DEFENSE (Word-for-Word Answers)

### Question 1: "How exactly does your game fit the 'ONE ROOM' hackathon theme?"
**Answer:**
> *"Our interpretation of 'ONE ROOM' is both literal and emotional.  
> 1. **Literally:** The game is architected entirely within a single scene and a single set of four walls. There are zero scene transitions or doorway loads.  
> 2. **Player Agency:** The room does not change on a timer. It only collapses because the player physically chose to pick up and play Tape 2.  
> 3. **Contrast:** The room at the start and the room at the end are polar opposites — one is a warm mid-century haven of denial, and the other is a cold, barren confession booth."*

---

### Question 2: "What were the toughest technical challenges you solved in Unity?"
**Answer:**
> *"Three major things in Unity 6:  
> 1. **Zero-Load Transition:** Orchestrating a multi-stage procedural dissolve of over 600 room objects, spinning clock physics, and dynamic lighting state swaps without hitching frame rate.  
> 2. **Atmospheric Lighting:** Tuning our custom `CeilingLampManager` and directional sunlight so that lighting directly mirrors the player's psychological state.  
> 3. **Contextual Audio & Proximity:** Seamlessly syncing tape voiceovers, 3D spatial clock ticking, and proximity prompts without overlapping or breaking immersion."*

---

### Question 3: "Why did you choose psychological trauma instead of a standard monster?"
**Answer:**
> *"Anyone can throw a jump scare or a zombie at a player — you forget about it 10 seconds later. But when you realize that every mundane domestic item in the room — the clock, the desk, the tape — was a piece of a fractured memory hiding a tragedy, the player feels genuine emotional weight. That is what makes SINS unforgettable."*
